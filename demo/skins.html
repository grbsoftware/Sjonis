One markup, three skins

All three columns are stamped from the same <template>. The only difference is data-skin on the wrapper — no skeleton, theme or component rule differs. Change the theme below and all three re-derive: every edge here is mixed from whatever surface or line the theme supplies, so these are grammars rather than pictures of a particular decade.

bevel — what to look at. Corners are square and no surface casts a soft shadow — depth is drawn with a light edge on two sides and a dark edge on the other two. Press a button and it goes down rather than changing colour. The input is cut into the page: its edges are the raised ones reversed. Focus is a dotted rectangle inside the control, not a ring around it. None of that is reachable from a theme at any setting.

cushion — what to look at, and what to look for. The panel keeps the theme's own radius and its own surface colour; only the top and bottom edges move, and the depth is underneath rather than around. Now switch to dark and watch which edge is doing the work. In light mode it is the shaded bottom (4.6–4.7 against the ground); in dark mode that edge all but vanishes and the lit top takes over (1.28–1.33). One edge carries each mode, which is how a lit surface actually behaves — and it is why the left and right edges stay on the theme's plain line, so a surface still has a boundary when its carrying edge fades out.

The cost each skin pays. bevel moves every panel to a mid face, and then has to re-tune four families of ink against it — dim text, faint text, the accent, and the rims on categorical marks — plus give .ui-pill a sunken well to sit in, because a state hue printed as text on that face lands around 2.0 against a 4.5 bar. cushion pays none of that: its fill is --ui-surface, so every ink is meeting the ground it was validated against. That is the trade. Drawn depth costs a face; cast depth costs a compositor.
